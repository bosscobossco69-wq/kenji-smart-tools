Percent and percentage points

A percentage point is the plain difference between two percentages. If an interest rate goes from 4% to 5%, it rises by 1 percentage point, but the relative increase is 25%, because 1 is a quarter of 4. News reports and financial documents often use both, so check which one is meant.

Why increases and decreases do not cancel out

A percentage always refers to the number it is applied to. If 100 goes up by 50% it becomes 150. If 150 then goes down by 50% it becomes 75, which is 25% lower than the start. To undo an increase, you need a smaller percentage decrease than the original increase.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

Multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100. For example, 15% of 200 is 200 x 15 / 100 = 30.

How do I calculate percentage change?

Subtract the old value from the new value, divide by the old value, then multiply by 100. A positive result is an increase and a negative result is a decrease.

Why is percentage change not defined when the starting value is 0?

The formula divides by the starting value, and dividing by zero has no result. In that case you can describe the change in absolute terms instead.

What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

Percentage points describe the simple difference between two percentages, such as 4% to 5%. Percent change describes the relative difference, which in that example is 25%.

Why does a 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease not return to the start?

Each percentage is applied to a different number. 100 plus 50% is 150, and 50% of 150 is 75, so the final value is 75, not 100.

Can a percentage be more than 100%?

Yes. If X is larger than Y, X is more than 100% of Y. For example, 150 is 150% of 100.
