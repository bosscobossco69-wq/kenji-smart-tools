What each count means

  • Words: groups of text separated by whitespace.
  • Characters: every character, including spaces and line breaks.
  • Sentences: approximate count based on common sentence-ending punctuation.
  • Paragraphs: blocks separated by one or more blank lines.

How to use word counts when writing

Word counts are useful when preparing essays, reports, product descriptions, applications, and social posts with length limits. Paste your draft into the editor and review the word and character totals as you revise. If a publisher or platform defines its own counting rules, use that service's count as the final reference because hyphenated words, contractions, URLs, and language-specific text may be treated differently.

Words, characters, and characters without spaces

A word count estimates how many units of text are separated by whitespace. The character total includes letters, numbers, punctuation, spaces, and line breaks. Characters without spaces removes whitespace characters from that total, which is useful when a form or platform sets a character limit that excludes spaces. Check the rules of the service you are submitting to, because counting conventions can differ.

Estimate reading time

The reading-time estimate uses a simple average of 200 words per minute and rounds up to the next whole minute. It is a planning guide rather than a measurement of an individual reader's speed. Technical writing, unfamiliar terms, and careful proofreading may take longer.

For the sample text “Write clearly. Check every word.” the counter reports 5 words, 33 characters including spaces and punctuation, and 28 characters without whitespace. The exact sentence and paragraph totals depend on the punctuation and line breaks in your draft.

Sentence and paragraph counts are estimates

Sentence detection commonly relies on punctuation such as periods, question marks, and exclamation marks. Abbreviations, decimal numbers, initials, and quoted text can make automatic sentence counts differ from a human editor's count. Paragraphs are identified from blank-line separation, so consistent formatting helps produce a useful estimate.

Privacy when editing drafts

Counting is performed in your browser and the text is not submitted to the KENJI Smart Tools server. If you are working on a shared computer, close the page when finished and avoid leaving confidential drafts visible to other users.

Frequently asked questions

Is my text uploaded?

No. Counting is performed locally in your browser and the text is not sent to our server.

Why can sentence counts differ between tools?

Abbreviations, decimals, initials, and languages use punctuation differently. Sentence counts are estimates, so check the text manually when an exact count is required.
