How to calculate a tip and split a bill

Enter the bill amount in US dollars, choose a tip percentage, and enter the number of people sharing the bill. The calculator shows the tip amount, the total bill, and each person’s share.

Tip calculation formula

Tip = bill amount × tip percentage ÷ 100. Total = bill amount + tip. Each person pays the total divided by the number of people.

Example

For a $100 bill with a 15% tip shared by two people, the tip is $15, the total is $115, and each person pays $57.50.

Worked example: tip and bill split

Suppose a restaurant bill is 120 and you want to leave a 10% tip. The tip is 12 and the total bill becomes 132. If four people split the total equally, each person pays 33. If you prefer to split only the original bill and let each person contribute a separate tip, calculate those amounts separately before paying.

Choose a tip before splitting

Decide whether the tip should be calculated on the pre-tax amount or on the final amount shown by the restaurant. Practices vary by country and venue. If the bill already includes a service charge, check the receipt and local custom before adding another tip.

Rounding and unequal shares

Dividing a bill can produce fractions of the smallest currency unit. The final payment may need to be rounded to the nearest cent or other local minor unit. For unequal shares, this equal-split calculator may not match what each person ordered; agree on item totals and shared charges separately.

Currency and local customs

The calculator performs arithmetic on the numbers you enter. It does not convert currencies, determine whether a service charge is mandatory, or recommend a customary tip rate. Use the rate appropriate to the country, restaurant, and service you received.

Frequently asked questions

Can I split the bill between more than two people?

Yes. Enter the number of people sharing the bill to calculate an equal share per person.

Does this calculator support other currencies?

The current display uses US dollars (USD). The calculation itself is currency-neutral, so you can enter an amount in another currency and interpret the result in that same currency.
